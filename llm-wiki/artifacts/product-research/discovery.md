# GPU product directions: discovery and first screen

This is the discovery list and first screen of GPU-acceleration product directions for peacockdb, dated 2026-10-28. It is desk research only: no interviews, no measurements. It covers the ten named options and every direction the six research notes turned up. The researchers hit three gaps. First, the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, and the GitHub API rate limit was hit at the end. So Reddit, Stack Overflow, Discord and several engines' issue trackers were not searched. Second, no first-person forum post was found in which a user asks for GPU SQL in any of these directions. The one exception is a single HN comment about Spark shuffle cost ([HN 43964505](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43964505)) (accessed 2026-10-28). Third, several 2026 facts rest on secondary sources. One GTC 2026 attendee write-up is the only source for: NVIDIA's hiring of ex-Voltron staff, Sirius becoming a core DuckDB extension, Parquet scan taking 60–70% of GPU Presto runtime, the Airbnb Trino skew figure, and the HeavyAI open-source roadmap ([GTC 2026 notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html)) (accessed 2026-10-28). A NextBigFuture summary is the only source for the GTC 2026 cuDF adopter list (Databricks, Snowflake, Dell, Oracle, EDB) and the cuVS integration list ([NextBigFuture](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2026/03/nvidia-structured-data-is-the-ground-truth-of-ai-120-billion-structure-data-ecosystem.html)) (accessed 2026-10-28). Startup funding and headcount figures come from aggregators. Most speedup numbers are vendor claims. The yardstick is 22,830 non-test lines, so 2× is about 45,660 lines.

## Screen

Screen rules apply to discovered options only. A discovered option fails only on one of three rules: (a) there is no sign of anyone needing it, (b) it is plainly over budget (more than 2× the yardstick), or (c) it is not GPU-centred. Weak demand evidence still counts as a sign. Platform risk is never a fail reason. Named options always pass, but some are marked weak.

## Merges

- heavy-query-offload (it appears in two notes as heavy-query-offload and iceberg-tail-offload) goes into **lakehouse-iceberg**. Both describe a GPU box that runs the heavy tail of queries over the same Parquet/Iceberg data.
- kafka-window-sql-gpu and htap-gpu-replica go into **realtime-vram-cache**. Both mean real-time ingest (Kafka or CDC) into VRAM-resident data.
- query-batching-gpu-for-high-qps goes into **realtime-other**. It is the technique Pinot/Doris-style high-QPS use would need, and it is research-grade at 1–2× (estimate).
- sirius-style-pseudo-backend-for-uncovered-engine goes into **distributed-pluggable**. Sirius proved the pattern on Doris ([sirius PR #1842](https://github.com/sirius-db/sirius/pull/1842)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- df-gpu-offload-crate goes into **datafusion-plugin**.
- managed-gpu-etl goes into **spark-plugin**. Its evidence is Spark/Glue/EMR shuffle cost, and ParaQuery already sells managed GPU Spark ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/paraquery)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- hipdf-port, metal-local-sql and sql-on-tensor-runtimes go into **other-accelerators**. Metal (1.5–3×) and tensor runtimes (1.5–2.5×) would likely be over budget on their own (estimate). The tensor-runtime idea rests on TQP ([arXiv 2203.01877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01877)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- multimodal-lake-sql goes into **vector-sql**.
- asof-gpu, tick-asof-gpu and the feature-store point-in-time join idea become **asof-gpu**.
- ml-feature-prep-sql, trainer-side-sql-prep and gpu-resident-sql-for-ml ("SQL over data already on GPU") become **trainer-side-sql-prep**.
- gpu-scan-tier, gpu-decode-scan-service and vortex-gpu-native-format become **gpu-scan-tier**.
- gov-airgapped-analytics and airgapped-defense-analytics become **gov-airgapped**.
- gpu-workstation-analytics, desk-gpu-sql, polars-gpu-sql-strict, duckdb-gpu-ext and the two Sirius-as-base options (contribute-to-sirius, sirius-packaged-niche) become **workstation-gpu-sql**. "Build on Sirius" is a build route open to every option, not a product of its own.
- ai-data-curation-sql goes into **llm-corpus-sql**. Telecom-network-analytics goes into **telco-xdr-gpu**. pg-gpu-ext goes into **postgres-gpu-analytics**. gpu-telemetry-analytics goes into **datafusion-log-gpu**.

## Discovered options that passed

### storage-oem-engine
- What: an embeddable single-node GPU SQL/ETL engine that storage or AI-data-platform vendors run next to the data.
- Who: infrastructure vendors. NetApp bought DataPelago in July 2026 ([NetApp PR](https://s21.q4cdn.com/371534297/files/doc_news/NetApp-Acquires-DataPelago-Making-Data-AI-Ready-at-the-Infrastructure-Layer-2026.pdf)), HPE embedded Theseus ([VMblog](https://vmblog.com/bylines/voltron-data-launches-theseus-to-unlock-the-power-of-the-largest-data-sets-for-ai/)), and Dell's AI Data Platform is listed as a cuDF user ([NextBigFuture](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2026/03/nvidia-structured-data-is-the-ground-truth-of-ai-120-billion-structure-data-ecosystem.html)) (accessed 2026-10-28). The pain is making stored data "AI-ready" without moving it.
- Build: 0.5–1× the yardstick, about 11–23k lines, for object-store readers, an embedding API and spilling (estimate).
- Platform risk: high. NVIDIA gives cuDF integrations to the same vendors, and the rest may build in-house (estimate).

### gov-airgapped
- What: a small, auditable GPU SQL appliance for air-gapped and on-prem government and defence sites.
- Who: agencies and defence. Kinetica markets intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR) tracking and border anomaly detection, air-gapped and IL5 ([Kinetica defense](https://www.kinetica.com/solutions/defense-tech)). Voltron sold to government through Carahsoft ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/voltron-data-carahsoft-partner-bring-160000202.html)). In-Q-Tel led MapD's Series A ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEAVY.AI)) (accessed 2026-10-28). Pavlo says Kinetica "has been milking government contracts" ([Pavlo](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pavlo/blog/2026/01/2025-databases-retrospective.html)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- Build: 0.5–2× the yardstick, about 11–46k lines, plus compliance work that is not code. The notes disagree on size (estimate).
- Platform risk: low from NVIDIA. Kinetica is the incumbent, and the sales cycle is long against a 6-month runway (estimate).

### ab-metrics-gpu
- What: a GPU engine for A/B-test and metric pipelines, which are mostly wide group-bys and joins over event logs.
- Who: companies whose experiment metrics cost too much. Snap runs thousands of experiments a month on about 2,100 L4 GPUs, 4× faster at 76% lower cost ([NVIDIA Snap blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/snap-accelerated-data-processing/)) (accessed 2026-10-28). Mid-size buyers are not proven.
- Build: 0.5–1× the yardstick, about 11–23k lines, for a metric DSL to SQL and incremental runs (estimate).
- Platform risk: high. Snap did it with stock spark-rapids.

### idle-gpu-batch
- What: batch SQL that runs in off-peak windows on inference or training GPUs an organisation already owns, scheduled around their jobs.
- Who: GPU owners with spare capacity. Clusters run at "30-40% effective utilization" ([Expanse Launch HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48356312)). Snap reportedly reuses idle inference GPUs overnight; this is unverified and comes from a podcast ([Podbean](https://www.podbean.com/ew/dir-ytehw-2db39e2c)). H100 contract rates rose about 40% from October 2025 to March 2026, which favours hardware that is already paid for ([Akash](https://akash.network/the-bid/h100-rental-price-2026-cost-per-hour/)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- Build: about 0.5× the yardstick, about 11k lines, for SLURM/Kubernetes integration, preemption and spilling (estimate).
- Platform risk: medium. No clear incumbent, but the demand evidence is indirect (estimate).

### llm-corpus-sql
- What: GPU SQL with built-in exact-hash, MinHash/LSH and n-gram decontamination operators, plus fast JSONL/Parquet scans.
- Who: mid-size teams curating 0.1–10 TB of fine-tuning or synthetic data. People query Hub datasets in SQL ([HF SQL Console](https://huggingface.co/blog/sql-console)). Dolma filtering costs about 122 CPU-hours per TB ([arXiv 2402.00159](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.00159)). One LLM provider found standalone dedup pipelines costly ([Zilliz](https://zilliz.com/blog/data-deduplication-at-trillion-scale-solve-the-biggest-bottleneck-of-llm-training)) (accessed 2026-10-28). Daft is used by AI labs for this kind of work ([Daft blog](https://www.daft.ai/blog/eventual-raises-30m-to-build-the-future-of-data)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- Build: 0.4–0.7× the yardstick, about 9–16k lines, for text only. Multimodal Lance support would take it to 1–2× (estimate).
- Platform risk: high. NeMo Curator does GPU dedup for free ([NeMo dedup docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/curator/curate-text/process-data/deduplication)) (accessed 2026-10-28).

### trainer-side-sql-prep
- What: SQL-defined filters, joins and encodings that run on the training GPU over tensors or Arrow data already in device memory. Results go to PyTorch via DLPack with no host round trip.
- Who: recsys and tabular-DL teams with GPUs starved for data. Meta's data stalls cut throughput by up to 56% ([arXiv 2108.09373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.09373)). Ray Data and Daft ran at under 20% GPU utilisation ([Anyscale](https://www.anyscale.com/blog/ray-data-daft-benchmarking-multimodal-ai-workloads)). Daft measured a 200–700× host round-trip tax ([Daft #7374](https://github.com/Eventual-Inc/Daft/issues/7374)). NVTabular's last release was in 2023 ([NVTabular](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Merlin/NVTabular)) (accessed 2026-10-28). No named team was found.
- Build: 0.4–1× the yardstick, about 9–23k lines, for a dataloader API, the DLPack hand-off and shuffling (estimate).
- Platform risk: medium-high. cudf-polars, Ray Data and Daft could each cover it (estimate).

### asof-gpu
- What: a GPU as-of and point-in-time join engine over Parquet, with sorts, window aggregates and OHLC bars.
- Who: two groups.
  - Quant teams priced out of kdb+ ("six figures per year", [techinterview](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474600/time-series-databases-quant/)). KX chose as-of join and sort as the operations worth moving to GPU and sought design partners ([KX blog](https://kx.com/?p=14992)).
  - Feature-store users. Tecton calls point-in-time training-set generation "fairly tricky" ([Tecton docs](https://docs.tecton.ai/docs/reading-feature-data/reading-feature-data-for-training/constructing-training-data)), the join "can take hours" ([apxml](https://apxml.com/courses/feature-stores-for-ml/chapter-3-data-consistency-quality/point-in-time-correctness)), and Tecton was absorbed into Databricks ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/tecton-joining-databricks-power-real-time-data-personalized-ai-agents)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- Build: 0.3–0.8× the yardstick, about 7–18k lines, for ASOF JOIN planning, a GPU as-of kernel and time-series functions (estimate).
- Platform risk: medium. KX KDB-X GPU went GA in April 2026 at the high end ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260402375288/en/)) (accessed 2026-10-28).

### datafusion-log-gpu
- What: drop-in GPU execution for DataFusion-based log lakes. It covers regex/LIKE filters, time-bucket group-bys, top-k and distinct counts over a pinned hot window.
- Who: operators of OpenObserve, Parseable and IceGate, which all run on DataFusion ([DataFusion known users](https://datafusion.apache.org/user-guide/introduction.html)). The pain is Datadog-style ingest, retention and search pricing ([OneUptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-03-18-the-real-cost-of-datadog/markdown)). Regex is a major log-analysis cost ([arXiv 2510.10348](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10348.pdf)). GPU-cloud operators also struggle with high-cardinality GPU telemetry ([Coupang job](https://www.coupang.jobs/jp/jobs/7992094/sr-staff-observability-engineer-gpu-cloud-telemetry-platform/)) (accessed 2026-10-28). Nobody was found asking for GPU here specifically.
- Build: 0.5–1× the yardstick, about 11–23k lines, for string/regex functions, a hot-window cache and object-store ingest (estimate).
- Platform risk: low from NVIDIA. CPU engines may be "good enough", since cold search is I/O-bound ([Splunk community](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Splunk-Search/Does-Splunk-support-CUDA/m-p/90446)) (accessed 2026-10-28).

### flow-hunt-gpu

- Platform risk: low now that Morpheus is End of Support ([NVIDIA EOL](https://docs.nvidia.com/ai-enterprise/lifecycle/latest/eol-notices.html)) (accessed 2026-10-28). SIEM vendors' CPU lakes are the main competition.

### gpu-scan-tier
- What: a GPU-native Parquet/Iceberg scan, decode and cache service, possibly transcoding into a GPU-friendly format, that feeds CPU or GPU engines.
- Who: anyone running GPU SQL, because decode dominates. The cuDF Parquet reader runs 10× below the I/O rate ([arXiv 2606.24647](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24647v1)). Scan is 60–70% of GPU Presto runtime ([GTC 2026 notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html)). ClickHouse transcodes at pin time because LZ4/ZSTD do not pay on GPU ([RFC #116807](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/issues/116807)) (accessed 2026-10-28).
- Build: 0.5–1.5× the yardstick, about 11–34k lines (estimate).
- Platform risk: high. NVIDIA owns the cuDF readers and nvcomp, and plans a Vortex reader ([GTC 2026 notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html)) (accessed 2026-10-28).

### workstation-gpu-sql
