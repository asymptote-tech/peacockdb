# GPU SQL / analytics database landscape (alive and dead) and product directions for peacockdb

Research note: the session's web-search budget ran out partway through. Later facts come from direct fetches of known primary URLs and from `gh api` on GitHub repos. The Gaps sections list what this left unverified. All links were accessed 2026-10-28.

## Q1. Which GPU database companies/projects exist or existed, what they sell, to whom, and their 2025-2026 status

### Takeaway
The independent GPU-database vendors have mostly died or been absorbed into NVIDIA. HeavyAI was acquired by NVIDIA in 2025 and fully open-sourced in Oct 2026. Voltron Data/Theseus cut half its staff in 2024, and its engineers now appear as NVIDIA staff working on Sirius. BlazingSQL closed in 2021 and Brytlyt was acquired in 2024. Kinetica (defense/real-time) and SQream (large on-prem ETL, "AI factory") still sell. Everything else is now an "accelerate an existing engine in place" project with NVIDIA engineers at its centre: Spark RAPIDS, Velox/Presto cuDF, Gluten GPU, Sirius for DuckDB/Doris, and cudf-polars.

**Sirius (DuckDB, Doris; UW-Madison + NVIDIA)**
- Sirius is a GPU-native engine that runs as a DuckDB extension. It consumes Substrait plans and is built on libcudf/RMM/NCCL. Apache-2.0. The CIDR 2026 paper claims 8.3× cost-efficiency on TPC-H and 7.4× on ClickBench versus single-node DuckDB. As a Doris accelerator on 4× A100 nodes it gives 2.4-12.5× at TPC-H SF100. — [arXiv 2508.04701](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04701v3); [CIDR'26 paper](https://vldb.org/cidrdb/papers/2026/p12-yogatama.pdf); [CIDR slides](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2026/slides/Yu-52.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- NVIDIA blog (15 Dec 2025): ClickBench record on a GH200 at $1.50/h versus CPU boxes at $7.30-9.80/h. NVIDIA "plans to integrate its own shareable GPU data-processing building blocks into Sirius". The roadmap covers spilling, GPU-native file readers, pipeline execution and multi-node. — [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-gpu-accelerated-sirius-achieves-record-setting-clickbench-record/) (accessed 2026-10-28)

### Inferences
- Failure pattern (estimate, from the evidence above). Standalone GPU databases had to replace a whole warehouse, so they sold slowly into government and big enterprise, often on-prem. When NVIDIA made its libraries free and pushed "accelerate in place", each company's moat shrank to cuDF plus glue. BlazingSQL folded into Voltron, Voltron missed revenue and its key engineers ended up at NVIDIA, and HeavyAI was acquired and given away. The survivors sell a vertical bundle (Kinetica: defense, geospatial and real-time) or a large on-prem ETL appliance (SQream), not raw SQL speed.
- NVIDIA is now the de facto owner of the GPU SQL stack: cuDF, Sirius (DuckDB-blessed), Velox/Presto with IBM, Spark plugin, cudf-polars, HeavyDB (free), and the Theseus lineage through cuCascade/RapidsMPF. Any direction that is "GPU SQL engine for X" where X is a popular open engine already has an NVIDIA-staffed project.
- In every serious 2025-2026 measurement the scan/decode/IO path is the bottleneck (Presto 60-70%, the user's spark-rapids 10×, the S3 bandwidth cap, Sirius' own pinned cache and O_DIRECT reader). Operator kernels are a solved commodity in libcudf.

### Gaps
- Databricks (Photon on GPU?), BigQuery, Oracle HeatWave/AI Database GPU SQL, and AWS Glue/Athena/Redshift GPU: no primary source was found before the search budget ran out. The adopter list above is secondary.
- Apache DataFusion Comet + GPU and Crystal (SIGMOD 2020) were not researched (search budget). From memory only: Crystal is a tile-based GPU query library from UW-Madison by Shanbhag et al. (estimate, unverified here).
- No primary source on the terms or date of the HeavyAI acquisition, or on whether Voltron Data was acquired, wound down or still trades. The NVIDIA staffing of Patterson and the Aramburus comes from one attendee blog plus GitHub contributor logins.
- Theseus' ~14 customers are unnamed. Kinetica and SQream 2025-2026 revenue, funding and named customers were not found.
- Non-NVIDIA SQL accelerators (Speedata APU, NeuroBlade SPU), Chinese GPU databases (Transwarp, Zilliz-adjacent) and others such as Bodo were not researched (search budget).

## Q2. What NVIDIA itself ships or announced in 2025-2026 for data processing (platform risk)

### Takeaway
NVIDIA has become the vendor of GPU SQL. It funds or staffs an accelerator for every major open engine: Spark, Presto/Velox, DuckDB/Doris (Sirius), Polars and Postgres via EDB. It owns HeavyDB and gave it away in Oct 2026. It is building shared infrastructure (cuCascade, RapidsMPF, GPU readers, nvcomp, a Vortex reader) aimed precisely at the scan/memory-tier problems a small engine would try to differentiate on. GTC 2026 presented cuDF and cuVS as the "structured/unstructured data" base of the enterprise-AI stack.

### Cited Findings
- GTC 2026 keynote (Mar 2026): Jensen Huang spent about 20 minutes on enterprise AI and showed cuDF and cuVS at the base of the stack. cuDF is said to accelerate Spark, Presto, DuckDB, Polars, Databricks, Snowflake, Starburst and EDB Postgres. Spark+cuDF claims up to 5× faster and 10× TCO at 10 TB. — [jazracherif GTC notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html); [NextBigFuture](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2026/03/nvidia-structured-data-is-the-ground-truth-of-ai-120-billion-structure-data-ecosystem.html) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- NVIDIA's own "accelerate existing SQL engines in place" page (updated 2026-08-18) names Spark, Presto via Velox, Polars, and DuckDB via Sirius, all with CPU fallback. Named results: Nestlé on watsonx.data (5× faster, 83% cheaper) and Snap (4× faster, 76% cheaper). — [NVIDIA perspectives](https://perspectives.nvidia.com/cudf/accelerating-existing-sql-engines-in-place-for-faster-query/) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- cuCascade: GPU memory-reservation and tiering library from the Theseus "MICE" design, "built from scratch starting January 1, 2026". It was used to stop TPC-H Q9 OOM at SF1K. Talk quote: cuDF "itself is not intended to be a multi-GPU library", and cuCascade adds topology-aware multi-GPU. The libcudf roadmap includes nvcomp integration and Vortex file-format support. A custom Rust telemetry layer has under 0.1% overhead. — [jazracherif GTC notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html); [github.com/NVIDIA/cuCascade](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cuCascade) (pushed 2026-10-28, 63 stars, via `gh api`) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- RapidsMPF: an NVIDIA streaming/multi-GPU execution library. cudf-polars 26.06 replaced its 25.06 Dask streaming executor with a RapidsMPF backend. PDS-H SF1000 on 1× B200 took 84.7 s versus 271.9 s on CPU Polars. SF3K on 8× B200 was 23.2× faster. It spills beyond VRAM. — [pola.rs "GPU streaming backend", 2026-08-28](https://pola.rs/posts/gpu-streaming-backend); [cudf-polars docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/cudf/latest/cudf_polars/) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Sirius is NVIDIA-staffed. The top contributors (`bwyogatama` 310 commits, `mbrobbel`, `felipeblazing`, `wmalpica`, `aminaramoon`, `mike-wendt`) include NVIDIA and ex-Voltron engineers. — [sirius-db/sirius contributors via gh api](https://github.com/sirius-db/sirius) (accessed 2026-10-28)

## Q3. Postgres-side GPU (PG-Strom, others): who uses it, what for

### Takeaway
PG-Strom is the only living open-source GPU Postgres extension. It is maintained by HeteroDB (Japan) and targets batch/analytic scans, joins and GROUP BY over large tables, with GPUDirect from NVMe and Arrow files. No named users were found. Brytlyt (a Postgres fork, later a MariaDB engine) was absorbed by a telecom-analytics firm. NVIDIA lists EDB Postgres as a cuDF adopter.

### Cited Findings
- PG-Strom: a PostgreSQL 15+ extension for "data analytics or batch processing". It GPU-accelerates WHERE scans, joins and GROUP BY. GPUDirect SQL reads NVMe/NVMe-oF directly into the GPU. Arrow_Fdw provides columnar Arrow files ("not suitable for update per row basis"). A GPU cache mirrors frequently updated tables for "real-time locational information" queries. Community support is "best effort and no SLA", and commercial support comes from HeteroDB, Inc. Latest listed release is v6.1. — [PG-Strom docs](https://heterodb.github.io/pg-strom/) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- v6.0 (1 Apr 2025) added GPU-Sort and better scheduling, and requires CUDA 12.2+. — [PG-Strom v6.0 release notes](https://heterodb.github.io/pg-strom/release_v6.0/); [KaiGai's May 2025 talk](https://speakerdeck.com/kaigai/pg-strom-v6-dot-0-xin-ji-neng-tosonoxian-nowei-lai) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- The repo is active (pushed 2026-10-28, 1,408 stars). — [heterodb/pg-strom](https://github.com/heterodb/pg-strom) via `gh api` (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Brytlyt was a PG 9.4 fork with GPU joins and aggregation, acquired by Opensignal in 2024 (citation flagged). — [dbdb.io Brytlyt](https://dbdb.io/db/brytlyt) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- EDB Postgres is listed among cuDF adopters at GTC 2026 (secondary). Google AlloyDB and pgvector-style workloads use cuVS (9× on HNSW), which is vector search, not SQL. — [NextBigFuture](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2026/03/nvidia-structured-data-is-the-ground-truth-of-ai-120-billion-structure-data-ecosystem.html) (accessed 2026-10-28)

### Inferences
- The likely PG-Strom users are Japanese IoT, log and telecom shops that keep large append-only log tables in Postgres (estimate, from the feature set: Arrow_Fdw, GPUDirect from NVMe, a location cache). This was not confirmed.
- A "Postgres-native GPU analytics" product has a real hook: teams that will not leave Postgres but run big scans. PG-Strom shows the demand is small but persistent. EDB plus cuDF could close the space. (estimate)

### Gaps
- No named PG-Strom/HeteroDB customers were found. A Japanese-language search (ascii.jp, HeteroDB case studies) was not done because the search budget ran out.
- What EDB actually ships with cuDF was not verified.

## Q4. Niches where survivors win, and directions not on the existing list

### Takeaway
Survivors win where the customer buys a vertical outcome under hard deployment constraints, not raw SQL speed:
- Kinetica: defense/intel real-time geospatial and track fusion, air-gapped, GovCloud, IL5.
- HeavyAI (historically): telecom network planning with RF simulation, and interactive geospatial dashboards on billions of rows.
- SQream: very large on-prem ETL in telecom, semiconductor and manufacturing, plus "data prep for AI".
- Brytlyt: ended up inside a mobile-network analytics firm.
- The NVIDIA-led projects win where a big CPU bill already exists and the engine can stay unchanged: Snap Spark, Nestlé Presto, Airbnb-style Trino skew.
- The consistent technical pain is I/O and decode (Parquet scan 60-70%, S3 caps), not operators.

### Cited Findings
- Telecom: HeavyRF (RF propagation plus terrain, clutter and tower data in one GPU view) is now free inside HeavyDB. Verizon invested in MapD and is a Kinetica logo. Brytlyt went to Opensignal. SQream lists telecom first among its segments. — [heavydb README](https://github.com/heavyai/heavydb); [Wikipedia HEAVY.AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEAVY.AI); [kinetica.com](https://www.kinetica.com/); [dbdb Brytlyt](https://dbdb.io/db/brytlyt); [sqream.com](https://sqream.com/) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Defense/public sector: Kinetica's ISR entity tracking, border anomaly detection, air-gapped deployment and IL5. HeavyAI's Series A was led by In-Q-Tel. — [Kinetica defense](https://www.kinetica.com/solutions/defense-tech); [Wikipedia HEAVY.AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEAVY.AI) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Finance fraud and risk: Kinetica lists "real-time fraud and risk detection", with Citi among its logos. BlazingSQL started on pension-fraud joins. — [kinetica.com](https://www.kinetica.com/); [dbdb BlazingSQL](https://dbdb.io/db/blazingsql) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Query skew: at Airbnb, 2% of Trino queries consume 92% of cluster resources (cited in an NVIDIA GTC 2026 talk). — [jazracherif GTC notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html) (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Scan bottleneck and cloud I/O: Parquet scan is 60-70% of Presto-GPU runtime. Delta-binary-packed integer encoding plus NUMA pinning gave about 30%. There is an undocumented per-VM S3 cap on AWS. Sirius gets about 2× throughput from its own pinned compressed cache. — [jazracherif GTC notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html) (accessed 2026-10-28)

- Data prep for ML: Snowflake ships cuDF/cuML for ML only. SQream sells "in-database training" and an "AI factory". Voltron bought Claypot (real-time features). — [Snowflake blog](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/nvidia-gpu-acceleration/); [SQream PR 2024](https://prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/sqream-continues-to-push-the-boundaries-of-innovation-ushering-in-a-new-era-of-big-data-analytics-with-gpu-enabled-in-database-model-training-302113138.html); [VentureBeat Claypot](https://venturebeat.com/data-infrastructure/exclusive-voltron-data-acquires-claypot-to-unlock-real-time-ai-with-modular-data-systems) (accessed 2026-10-28)

DuckDB 2.0 lists `vortex` as a core extension. — [jazracherif GTC notes](https://jazracherif.github.io/nvidia/gtc/analytics/gpu/2026/04/09/accelerated-analytics-at-gtc-2026-part1-technical-deep-dives.html); [DuckDB 2.0 core extensions](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/core_extensions/overview.html) (accessed 2026-10-28)

Snowflake's GPUs are for ML, not SQL. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/synapse-analytics/spark/apache-spark-gpu-concept); [AWS EMR docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/emr/latest/ReleaseGuide/emr-spark-rapids.html); [Snowflake blog](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/nvidia-gpu-acceleration/) (accessed 2026-10-28)

### Inferences
- Even NVIDIA's own showcase wins (Snap, Nestlé) are large companies with a big existing CPU bill and an existing engine. A 1-3 person team cannot sell to them directly. It can only sell into a niche where the engine itself is the product or the deployment constraint is unusual. (estimate)
- peacockdb's assets are a DataFusion/Rust frontend, its own wire format, a single-node cuDF executor and Parquet row-group pruning. They are closest to DataFusion-based products (not covered by NVIDIA), to scan-path work, and to embedded or vertical deployments.
